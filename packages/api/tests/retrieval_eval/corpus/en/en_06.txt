Minutes of the Transport Committee, Regular Meeting

Present: {{PII:PERSON_NAME:1}} (chair), {{PII:PERSON_NAME:2}} (vice chair), five committee members, the head of the roads department, and two representatives of {{PII:ORGANIZATION_NAME:1}}. Apologies were received from one member. The meeting opened at 18:30 in the council chamber, and the minutes were taken by the committee secretary.

1. Minutes of the previous meeting. The minutes were approved without changes after the chair corrected the spelling of one street name.

2. Cycle lane on the river road. The head of the roads department presented the revised design for a protected cycle lane along the river road. The plan removes forty-two on-street parking spaces and adds a raised kerb between the lane and traffic. Several members worried about the shops at the northern end, which depend on short-stay parking. The department proposed to offset the loss with twelve new bays in the former market yard, ten minutes away on foot. After discussion the committee voted six to one to approve the design, subject to a six-month review of trading figures for the affected shops. The dissenting member asked that her objection be recorded because she considers the replacement parking too far from the shop entrances.

3. Night bus service. The committee reviewed the trial of the night bus on route 14, which ran on Friday and Saturday nights for four months. Average loadings were nineteen passengers per trip, below the target of twenty-five, but the operator reported that the service reduced taxi demand outside the station and received positive comments from hospital staff finishing late shifts. A member suggested moving the last departure forty minutes earlier to match the end of cinema screenings. The committee decided to extend the trial by three months with the new timetable and to ask the operator for monthly passenger counts by stop.

4. School crossing at the primary school. Parents had submitted a petition asking for a signalised crossing on the hill road. Traffic counts showed that the average speed of cars is thirty-eight kilometres per hour although the limit is thirty. The chair stated that a full signal installation would cost around ninety thousand euros and could not be built before next year. As an interim measure the committee agreed to ask for a temporary speed camera trailer for four weeks, painted warning markings, and a crossing guard during the morning and afternoon peaks.

5. Budget. The finance officer reported that the committee has spent sixty-one percent of its annual budget at the three-quarter point. The cycle lane would require a transfer of fifty thousand euros from the reserve for road resurfacing, which was agreed in principle, to be confirmed by the full council.

6. Any other business. One member asked for a progress report on the e-scooter rental scheme. The vice chair promised to put it on the agenda of the next meeting. Another member asked that complaints can be sent to {{PII:EMAIL_ADDRESS:1}}, and the secretary will add the address to the committee page.

Actions. The roads department prepares the six-month review framework for the cycle lane. The operator supplies passenger counts. The secretary writes to the school and the petition organisers. The meeting closed at 20:40, and the next meeting will take place four weeks later.

Public comments. Three residents spoke before the vote on item 2. A shop owner from the northern end asked whether the council would compensate for lost trade during construction, and the chair replied that the council has no such scheme but that the roads department will keep one lane open for deliveries until the work is finished. A cycling campaigner welcomed the design and asked that the surface be swept more often in autumn, since fallen leaves make the lane slippery. A resident of the hill road described near-collisions at the school crossing and thanked the committee for acting on the petition.

Declarations of interest. One member declared that her sister owns a bakery on the river road and did not take part in the discussion on the parking bays, although she voted on the main item. The secretary noted that the committee's code of conduct requires abstention only when a member has a direct financial interest, which was not the case here.

Documents circulated. The revised cycle lane drawings, the night bus passenger report, the traffic count summary for the hill road, and the quarterly budget statement were sent to members five days before the meeting. Printed copies were available at the door for members of the public.
