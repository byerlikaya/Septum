Travel and Expense Guidelines for Employees

These guidelines apply to everyone employed by {{PII:ORGANIZATION_NAME:1}} who travels on company business. They are meant to be simple: spend company money as carefully as you would spend your own, and keep the paperwork needed to prove it.

Booking travel. All flights, trains and hotels are booked through the company travel portal so that negotiated rates apply and so that we know where people are in case of an emergency. Book at least fourteen days ahead whenever possible, because late bookings typically cost thirty to fifty percent more. Economy class is the rule for flights under six hours. Business class may be approved for longer flights if the employee is expected to work immediately after landing, and the approval must come from the department head in advance. Trains are preferred over short flights when the journey takes less than four hours.

Hotels. The portal shows hotels within the nightly rate limit for each city. Choose a hotel close to the meeting place rather than the cheapest one far away, since taxi costs usually cancel the saving. Breakfast included in the room rate is fine, but minibar items, films and spa visits are personal costs. If a conference organiser offers a special rate, you may book it directly, provided you attach the offer to your expense claim.

Meals and daily allowance. We pay a fixed daily allowance for meals while travelling instead of reimbursing each receipt. The amount depends on the country and is listed on the intranet. If a client or partner pays for a meal, the allowance for that meal is deducted. Alcohol is never reimbursed, even at client dinners, unless the finance director agreed beforehand.

Local transport. Public transport is the default. A taxi is reasonable late at night, with heavy luggage, or when the alternative would waste substantial working time. Hire cars require approval and must be of the compact class. Private cars used for business are reimbursed per kilometre at the rate set each January, and the claim must show the start, destination and distance.

Expense claims. Submit your claim within thirty days after the trip through the finance application. Attach photographs of receipts that show date, supplier, amount and tax. Claims without receipts are returned unless the amount is below ten euros. Your manager approves, and finance pays the approved amount into the account that you have registered, normally within ten days. If you paid with a company card, the statement is matched with your receipts monthly, and unmatched items are reported to your manager.

Advances and cancellations. For trips with costs above one thousand euros, you can ask finance for an advance. Unused advances are repaid within two weeks after returning. If a trip is cancelled, cancel all bookings immediately and tell the travel desk at {{PII:EMAIL_ADDRESS:1}}, so that credits are not lost. Non-refundable tickets that go unused must be reported, as they can often be reused within a year.

Personal days and mixed trips. If you extend a business trip with private days, you pay for the additional hotel nights and any difference in fare. Please state clearly in the claim which days were private.

Questions and misuse. Ask the travel desk before you book if you are unsure. Deliberate misuse, such as claiming costs that were not incurred, is treated as a breach of contract and can lead to dismissal.

Conferences and training. Conference fees are paid by the company if the event was approved in the annual training plan. Register early to use discounts, and ask the organiser for an invoice in the company name rather than paying personally. Travel time to a training course counts as working time up to the normal daily hours; time beyond that is compensated with time off in agreement with your manager. If you do not attend a course you registered for, tell the training desk at once, because cancellation fees often rise sharply in the last week.

Foreign currency and cards. When you pay in a foreign currency, choose to be charged in the local currency instead of accepting the conversion offered at the terminal, since the terminal rate is usually worse. Finance converts claims at the rate on the day of payment. Cash withdrawals with a company card are discouraged and need an explanation in the claim.

Loyalty points. Points earned on business trips may be kept by the employee, but they may not influence the choice of airline or hotel. Where a cheaper option exists, it must be selected.

Safety. If you feel unsafe in a location, change your plans and tell your manager. The company covers the cost of a taxi or a better-located hotel in such cases without any questions. In case of an emergency abroad, the travel desk can be reached around the clock via the number on the back of your staff card.
